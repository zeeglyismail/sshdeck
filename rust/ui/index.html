Large transfers

SFTP is the default and is the reliable path. Leave this off for anything that has to be correct — database dumps, disk images, backups.

Known problem: on some hosts a streamed transfer can end up short by roughly the size of one SSH window (a couple of MB) even though every byte was sent, and the transfer may hang at the very end. The size is always checked afterwards, so a short transfer is reported as failed rather than passed off as complete — but the speed is not worth the risk unless you are moving something you can re-check yourself.

SFTP waits for an acknowledgement every 512 KB, so its ceiling is 512 KB ÷ round-trip time — about 16 MB/s at 30 ms no matter how fast the link is. Streaming removes that ceiling. SSHDeck also samples the file and turns on zstd compression when it actually shrinks (database dumps do, packed archives do not), and writes sparsely so a mostly-empty disk image stays a hole on the destination. Needs zstd, GNU dd and tail on the host; anything missing falls back to SFTP automatically.
